# Golden Triangle monuments (Delhi, Agra, Jaipur)

Sourced claims for the corridor Zenny knows deepest. Prefer these over general knowledge.

## Agra Fort (Agra)

- (verified) UNESCO records Agra Fort as a powerful 16th-century red-sandstone fortress whose 2.5 km circuit of walls encloses the imperial city of the Mughal rulers, near the Taj Mahal gardens. Also: Red Fort of Agra. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/251
- (verified) Within the fort, the Diwan-i-Am (Hall of Public Audience) is the ceremonial hall where the emperor received petitions — part of the palatial sequence inside the walls. Also: Diwan-i-Am Agra, Hall of Public Audience Agra. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/251
- (verified) The Diwan-i-Khas (Hall of Private Audience) was reserved for receiving distinguished guests and conducting court in a more intimate palatial setting than the public hall. Also: Diwan-i-Khas Agra, Hall of Private Audience Agra. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/251
- (estimated) Musamman Burj is an octagonal marble tower-palace overlooking the Yamuna; later Mughal tradition associates it with Shah Jahan's confinement with a view toward the Taj Mahal. Also: Musamman Burj, Octagonal Tower. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/251
- (verified) Jahangiri Mahal is a large palatial block inside Agra Fort associated with Akbar-period residential architecture in red sandstone, contrasting with later white-marble additions. Also: Jahangiri Mahal. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/251
- (verified) Historic gates including the Delhi Gate and Amar Singh Gate control entry through the fort's long circuit of walls. Also: Amar Singh Gate, Delhi Gate Agra. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/251

## Agra Fort — Diwan-i-Am (Agra)

- Agra Fort's Diwan-i-Am is the public audience hall where the emperor received petitions, distinct from the marble Diwan-i-Khas on the river terrace. Also: Hall of Public Audience Agra Fort. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/251

## Agra Fort — Diwan-i-Khas (Agra)

- Agra Fort's Diwan-i-Khas is the marble private-audience hall on the river terrace, used for receiving nobles rather than the general public of the Diwan-i-Am. Also: Hall of Private Audience. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/251

## Agra Fort — Jahangiri Mahal (Agra)

- Jahangiri Mahal is a large Akbar-period red-sandstone palace block inside Agra Fort, contrasting with later white-marble pavilions on the river side. Also: Jahangiri Mahal rooms. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/251

## Agra Fort — Musamman Burj (Agra)

- Musamman Burj is the octagonal marble tower-palace on Agra Fort's Yamuna face; later tradition links it with Shah Jahan's confinement and a view toward the Taj Mahal. Also: Octagonal Tower Agra, Shah Jahan tower. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/251

## Ajanta Caves — Paintings (Aurangabad)

- Ajanta's later caves preserve Buddhist wall paintings of Jataka stories and courtly scenes; UNESCO cites the paintings and sculptures as masterpieces of Buddhist religious art. Also: Ajanta murals, Cave 1 Ajanta. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/242

## Ajanta — Cave 26 chaitya (Aurangabad)

- Ajanta Cave 26 is a later Mahayana chaitya hall with a carved stupa and extensive sculpture, part of the painted-and-cut Buddhist gorge UNESCO lists. Also: Ajanta Cave 26. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/242

## Amber Fort (Jaipur)

- (verified) Amber Fort is one of the Hill Forts of Rajasthan inscribed by UNESCO; its palaces are reached through ceremonial gates such as Ganesh Pol, with mirrored Sheesh Mahal interiors in the upper palace. Also: Amer Fort, Ganesh Pol, Sheesh Mahal Amber. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247
- (verified) The fort's processional courts include Suraj Pol and a Diwan-i-Am, organizing Rajput-Mughal court life on the hill above Maota Lake. Also: Suraj Pol, Diwan-i-Am Amber. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247

## Amber Fort — Diwan-i-Am court (Jaipur)

- Amber Fort's Diwan-i-Am sits in the processional courts above Suraj Pol, organising Rajput-Mughal public audience on the hill over Maota Lake. Also: Amber public audience. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247

## Amber Fort — Ganesh Pol (Jaipur)

- Ganesh Pol is a ceremonial painted gateway on Amber Fort's processional climb into the palace courts, part of the UNESCO Hill Forts of Rajasthan palace-fort sequence. Also: Ganesh Gate Amber, Ganesh Pol. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247

## Amber Fort — Sheesh Mahal (Jaipur)

- The Sheesh Mahal at Amber is a mirrored palace chamber whose glass inlay was designed to multiply lamplight, part of the upper palace apartments of the Kachwaha court. Also: Mirror Palace Amber. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247

## Brihadisvara — Vimana (Thanjavur)

- The Brihadisvara Temple's granite vimana is the vertical marker of the Chola imperial temple at Thanjavur, part of UNESCO's Great Living Chola Temples. Also: Thanjavur vimana, Rajarajesvaram tower. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/250

## Brihadisvara — inner prakara (Thanjavur)

- Brihadisvara's inner prakara and subsidiary shrines wrap the granite vimana; it is a living Chola temple with worship hours, part of UNESCO's Great Living Chola Temples. Also: Thanjavur courtyard. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/250

## CST — Victorian Gothic terminus (Mumbai)

- Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus combines Victorian Gothic Revival with Indian architectural motifs and remains a working railway terminus, as UNESCO records. Also: Victoria Terminus architecture. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/945

## Capitol Complex — Open Hand (Chandigarh)

- The Open Hand Monument is the emblematic sculpture of Chandigarh's Capitol Complex, part of the UNESCO-listed Le Corbusier ensemble with the Assembly and High Court. Also: Open Hand Monument. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1321

## Charminar — four arches (Hyderabad)

- Charminar's four grand arches open onto the cardinal bazaars of Hyderabad's old city; an upper mosque level sits over the ceremonial monument of 1591. Also: Charminar mosque level. Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Chittorgarh — Kirti Stambha (Chittorgarh)

- Kirti Stambha (Tower of Fame) is a Jain-carved tower inside Chittorgarh Fort, distinct from the taller Vijay Stambha on the same plateau citadel. Also: Tower of Fame Chittor. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247

## Chittorgarh — Vijay Stambha (Chittorgarh)

- The Vijay Stambha (Tower of Victory) is a carved stone tower inside Chittorgarh Fort, a landmark of the Sisodia citadel on the UNESCO Hill Forts of Rajasthan list. Also: Tower of Victory Chittor. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247

## City Palace Jaipur — Chandra Mahal (Jaipur)

- Chandra Mahal is the principal residential palace tower of Jaipur's City Palace complex at the heart of the 1727 planned city. Also: Chandra Mahal. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1605

## City Palace, Jaipur (Jaipur)

- (estimated) The City Palace complex occupies the heart of the 1727 planned city and remains associated with the Kachwaha court, combining Chandra Mahal residences with later museum courts. Also: Chandra Mahal, Mubarak Mahal. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1605

## Elephanta — Trimurti (Mumbai)

- The colossal Trimurti (Maheshmurti) of Shiva is the principal sculpture of the Elephanta cave temple, reached by ferry from the Gateway of India when the harbour service runs. Also: Maheshmurti, Trimurti Elephanta. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/244

## Ellora Caves — Kailasa Temple (Aurangabad)

- Ellora Cave 16, the Kailasa temple, is a monolithic excavation representing Mount Kailasa, carved downward from a single basalt outcrop — the largest of the Ellora monuments. Also: Cave 16 Ellora. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/243

## Ellora — Cave 10 chaitya (Aurangabad)

- Ellora Cave 10, the Vishvakarma chaitya, is a rock-cut Buddhist worship hall with a stupa and ribbed ceiling, distinct from the Hindu Kailasa (Cave 16). Also: Vishvakarma cave, Cave 10. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/243

## Ellora — Jain caves (Aurangabad)

- Ellora's southern Jain group (including the Indra Sabha, Cave 32) continues the cliff sequence beyond the Hindu and Buddhist caves, illustrating three faiths in one basalt face. Also: Ellora Cave 32, Indra Sabha. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/243

## Fatehpur Sikri (Agra)

- (verified) UNESCO notes Fatehpur Sikri was built in the second half of the 16th century by Akbar as a short-lived Mughal capital; the Jama Masjid and monumental Buland Darwaza dominate the sacred court. Also: Buland Darwaza, Jama Masjid Fatehpur. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/255
- (verified) The palatial zone includes distinctive structures such as the Panch Mahal and the Diwan-i-Khas, whose planning expresses Akbar-period court ceremonial and experimental architecture. Also: Panch Mahal, Diwan-i-Khas Fatehpur, Ankh Michauli. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/255

## Fatehpur Sikri — Buland Darwaza (Agra)

- The Buland Darwaza is the monumental victory gate of Fatehpur Sikri's Jama Masjid court, among the largest gateway structures of Mughal India. Also: Buland Darwaza. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/255

## Fatehpur Sikri — Buland Darwaza climb (Agra)

- The Buland Darwaza is approached by a steep flight of steps from the town side into Fatehpur Sikri's mosque court — the victory gate of Akbar's Friday mosque. Also: Buland Darwaza steps. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/255

## Fatehpur Sikri — Diwan-i-Khas (Agra)

- Fatehpur Sikri's Diwan-i-Khas is a square hall with a central pillar and bridging galleries, associated with Akbar-period court discussion — a different building from Agra Fort's marble Diwan-i-Khas. Also: Diwan-i-Khas Fatehpur, Ibadat Khana pillar. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/255

## Fatehpur Sikri — Jodha Bai Palace (Agra)

- The large palatial zenana block at Fatehpur Sikri commonly called Jodha Bai's Palace is a self-contained residential court of Akbar-period red sandstone. Also: Jodha Bai ka Mahal, Shabistan-i-Iqbal. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/255

## Fatehpur Sikri — Panch Mahal (Agra)

- The Panch Mahal is a five-storey pillared palace pavilion in Fatehpur Sikri's court zone, an Akbar-period experimental structure open to the air. Also: Panch Mahal. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/255

## Gateway of India — arch (Mumbai)

- The Gateway of India's basalt arch on Apollo Bunder is the harbour ceremonial front and the Elephanta ferry dock, a 20th-century Indo-Saracenic monument. Also: Apollo Bunder arch. Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Goa — Basilica of Bom Jesus (Old Goa)

- The Basilica of Bom Jesus in Old Goa holds the relics of St Francis Xavier and is the best-known church of the UNESCO Churches and Convents of Goa ensemble. Also: Bom Jesus relics. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/234

## Golconda — Fateh Darwaza (Hyderabad)

- Fateh Darwaza is a principal gateway of Golconda Fort, associated with the citadel's famous acoustic-clap demonstration along the entry path. Also: Fateh Gate Golconda. Source: Archaeological Survey of India — https://asi.nic.in/

## Golden Temple — Amrit Sarovar (Amritsar)

- Harmandir Sahib stands in the Amrit Sarovar, the sacred tank that gives Amritsar its name; circumambulation of the marble parikrama is part of the gurdwara visit, with head covering required. Also: sarovar, holy tank Amritsar. Source: Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee — https://sgpc.net/

## Golden Temple — Parikrama marble (Amritsar)

- The marble parikrama around the Amrit Sarovar is the circumambulation path of Harmandir Sahib; head covering and washing of feet are part of the gurdwara discipline. Also: Darbar Sahib parikrama. Source: Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee — https://sgpc.net/

## Hampi — Stone Chariot (Hampi)

- The stone chariot in Hampi's Vittala Temple courtyard is a shrine-form ratha of the Vijayanagara sacred centre, among the most visited sculptures on the Tungabhadra floodplain. Also: Vittala chariot. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/241

## Hampi — Virupaksha Temple (Hampi)

- The Virupaksha Temple at Hampi is a living shrine of the Vijayanagara sacred centre, predating and outlasting the 14th–16th century imperial capital around it. Also: Virupaksha. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/241

## Hampi — Vittala Temple (Hampi)

- The Vittala Temple complex at Hampi includes the iconic stone chariot and colonnaded halls on the Tungabhadra floodplain, a highlight of the Vijayanagara sacred centre. Also: Stone Chariot, Vittala. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/241

## Hawa Mahal (Jaipur)

- (estimated) Hawa Mahal stands on the edge of Jaipur's walled city bazaar street as a five-storey facade of screened windows, built so royal women could observe street life while remaining unseen. Also: Palace of Winds. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1605

## Hawa Mahal — screened facade (Jaipur)

- Hawa Mahal's five-storey facade is a screen of small windows on the bazaar street, built so royal women could watch the city while remaining unseen. Also: Palace of Winds windows. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1605

## Humayun's Tomb (Delhi)

- (verified) UNESCO describes Humayun's Tomb as the first garden-tomb on the Indian subcontinent and a landmark in the development of Mughal architecture, set in a charbagh. Also: garden-tomb, charbagh Humayun. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/232
- (verified) The mausoleum's high double dome and red sandstone with marble inlay established a prototype that later Mughal tombs, including the Taj Mahal, developed further. Also: double dome. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/232

## Humayun's Tomb — Barber's Tomb (Delhi)

- The so-called Barber's Tomb (Nai ka Gumbad) is a smaller mausoleum in Humayun's garden-tomb precinct, a quieter satellite dome in the same charbagh landscape. Also: Nai ka Gumbad. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/232

## Humayun's Tomb — Garden charbagh (Delhi)

- Humayun's Tomb sits in a four-part Mughal garden (charbagh); UNESCO cites it as the first garden-tomb on the subcontinent, a prototype later developed at the Taj Mahal. Also: Humayun charbagh, Sunder Nursery neighbour. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/232

## Humayun's Tomb — Isa Khan garden-tomb (Delhi)

- Isa Khan's tomb and mosque, just before Humayun's west gate, is an earlier Sur-period garden-tomb in the same precinct, a quieter octagonal mausoleum than the main double dome. Also: Isa Khan Niyazi. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/232

## Humayun's Tomb — West Gate (Delhi)

- Humayun's Tomb is entered through a monumental west gate into the charbagh; the high double dome of the mausoleum is the visual centre of the garden-tomb. Also: Humayun gateway. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/232

## Jaisalmer Fort — living streets (Jaisalmer)

- Jaisalmer Fort is a living fort-city: palaces, Jain temples, and residential lanes sit inside the UNESCO Hill Forts of Rajasthan property, not an empty citadel. Also: Sonar Quila lanes. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247

## Jaisalmer — Jain temples in fort (Jaisalmer)

- A cluster of richly carved Jain temples stands inside Jaisalmer Fort's living streets, part of the UNESCO hill-fort city rather than a separate desert shrine. Also: Jaisalmer Jain temples. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247

## Jaisalmer — palace square (Jaisalmer)

- The palace cluster at the crest of Jaisalmer Fort looks over the living fort-city lanes; it is part of the UNESCO Hill Forts of Rajasthan property, still inhabited below. Also: Jaisalmer Palace, Fort palace. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247

## Jantar Mantar — Samrat Yantra (Jaipur)

- The Samrat Yantra at Jaipur's Jantar Mantar is the site's largest masonry sundial, one of the monumental astronomical instruments built by Sawai Jai Singh II. Also: Samrat Yantra Jaipur. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1338

## Jantar Mantar, Jaipur (Jaipur)

- (verified) UNESCO describes the Jaipur Jantar Mantar as an 18th-century astronomical observation site built by Sawai Jai Singh II, with monumental masonry instruments including the Samrat Yantra. Also: Samrat Yantra, Jai Prakash Yantra. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1338

## Khajuraho — Kandariya Mahadeva (Khajuraho)

- Kandariya Mahadeva is the largest surviving Chandela temple in the Khajuraho western group, a Nagara shikhara densely carved with sculptural bands. Also: Kandariya. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/240

## Khajuraho — Lakshmana Temple (Khajuraho)

- Lakshmana Temple in the western group is an earlier Chandela Nagara temple than Kandariya Mahadeva, still densely sculpted and on the same ASI ticketed lawn. Also: Lakshmana Khajuraho. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/240

## Konark — Audience Hall (Konark)

- The surviving mass of Konark Sun Temple includes the jagamohana (audience hall) of the chariot-temple; the towering sikhara over the sanctum is largely lost. Also: Jagamohana Konark. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/246

## Konark — Chariot Wheels (Konark)

- The Sun Temple is planned as Surya's chariot: twelve pairs of richly carved stone wheels along the platform are both symbolic calendar forms and structural ornament of the Kalinga vimana. Also: Konark wheels. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/246

## Konark — Natamandira platform (Konark)

- The natamandira (dance hall) platform in front of Konark's jagamohana is part of the chariot-temple's east-facing ritual sequence, now an open sculpted plinth. Also: Konark dance hall. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/246

## Kumbhalgarh — perimeter wall (Kumbhalgarh)

- Kumbhalgarh's fortification walls run for tens of kilometres across the Aravallis, enclosing palaces and temples — the defensive landscape of this UNESCO hill fort. Also: Kumbhalgarh walls. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247

## Lotus Temple — prayer hall (Delhi)

- The Lotus Temple's interior is a silent Baháʼí prayer hall under the lotus-form concrete shells, open to all with posted quiet-and-photography rules. Also: Lotus interior. Source: Baha'i House of Worship, New Delhi — https://www.bahaihouseofworship.in/

## Mahabalipuram — Arjuna's Penance (Mahabalipuram)

- The open-air rock relief often called Arjuna's Penance or the Descent of the Ganges is a Pallava narrative sculpture on the Mahabalipuram hill, part of the UNESCO group. Also: Descent of the Ganges, Bhagiratha relief. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/249

## Mahabalipuram — Five Rathas (Mahabalipuram)

- The Five Rathas (Pancha Rathas) are Pallava monolithic rock-cut shrines south of the Shore Temple, part of the UNESCO Mahabalipuram group, carved from single granite outcrops. Also: Pancha Rathas, Five Rathas. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/249

## Mahabalipuram — Krishna's Butter Ball (Mahabalipuram)

- Krishna's Butter Ball is a naturally perched granite boulder on the Mahabalipuram hill, a popular photo stop within the Pallava monument landscape, not a separate ticketed cave. Also: Butter Ball, Krishna's Butterball. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/249

## Mahabalipuram — Mahishasuramardini cave (Mahabalipuram)

- The Mahishasuramardini cave-temple on Mahabalipuram hill contains Pallava reliefs of Durga's battle and Vishnu reclining, part of the UNESCO group above the Shore Temple. Also: Mahishasuramardini mandapa. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/249

## Mahabalipuram — Shore Temple (Mahabalipuram)

- The Shore Temple is a structural stone temple on the Bay of Bengal, part of the Pallava group, dedicated primarily to Shiva and unusual as a surviving seaside Pallava sanctuary. Also: Shore Temple. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/249

## Mahabodhi — Bodhi Tree shrine (Bodh Gaya)

- The Mahabodhi complex marks the place of the Buddha's Enlightenment; the Bodhi Tree and stone vajrasana sit beside the towering brick temple that UNESCO lists. Also: Bodhi Tree, vajrasana. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1056

## Meenakshi — Gopurams (Madurai)

- Meenakshi Amman Temple is entered through soaring painted gopurams that mark the living temple-city of Madurai; it is a worship site with dress and photography rules, not a museum-only ruin. Also: Madurai gopuram, Meenakshi towers. Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Meenakshi — Thousand Pillar Hall (Madurai)

- The Thousand Pillar Hall (Aayiram Kaal Mandapam) is a vast colonnaded mandapa in the Meenakshi complex, a living-temple museum space with dress rules, not a secular gallery. Also: Aayiram Kaal Mandapam. Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Mehrangarh — palatial apartments (Jodhpur)

- Mehrangarh's inner palatial apartments (including halls such as Moti Mahal) are museum rooms inside the cliff fort above Jodhpur's blue town. Also: Mehrangarh palaces, Moti Mahal Jodhpur. Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Nahargarh Fort (Jaipur)

- (estimated) Nahargarh is one of the forts overlooking the walled city of Jaipur, forming part of the same hill-fort landscape as Amber in the UNESCO Hill Forts of Rajasthan series context for the region. Also: Tiger Fort. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247

## Pattadakal — Virupaksha Temple (Pattadakal)

- The Virupaksha Temple at Pattadakal is a major Early Chalukya structural temple on the Malaprabha, blending southern and northern forms in the UNESCO ensemble. Also: Pattadakal Virupaksha. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/239

## Qutb Minar (Delhi)

- (verified) UNESCO lists Qutb Minar and its Monuments as a complex begun in the late 12th century, including the minaret, the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque, and the Iron Pillar in the mosque courtyard. Also: Qutub Minar, Quwwat-ul-Islam, Iron Pillar. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/233
- (verified) The Alai Darwaza is the southern gateway of the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque, added under Alauddin Khalji, and is a key early example of true arcuate construction in the complex. Also: Alai Darwaza. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/233

## Qutb Minar — Iron Pillar (Delhi)

- The Iron Pillar in the Qutb mosque courtyard is a 4th-century inscribed metallurgical pillar, famous for its rust-resistant composition, standing among later Sultanate structures. Also: Iron Pillar of Delhi. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/233

## Qutb Minar — Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque (Delhi)

- The Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque is the congregational prayer court at the foot of Qutb Minar, begun in the late 12th century around the Iron Pillar. Also: Quwwat ul Islam. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/233

## Qutb — Alai Darwaza (Delhi)

- The Alai Darwaza is the southern sandstone-and-marble gateway of the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque at the Qutb, added under Alauddin Khalji. Also: Alai Gate. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/233

## Qutb — Tomb of Iltutmish (Delhi)

- The tomb of Iltutmish at the Qutb is a square chamber of carved red sandstone added beside the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque, an early Sultanate funerary hall. Also: Iltutmish tomb. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/233

## Rani-ki-Vav — sculpted levels (Patan)

- Rani-ki-Vav is an inverted-temple stepwell whose descending levels are lined with sculptural panels, primarily Vaishnava, in the Maru-Gurjara style. Also: stepwell galleries. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/922

## Rani-ki-Vav — well shaft (Patan)

- The lowest levels of Rani-ki-Vav reach the well shaft, an inverted-temple descent through sculpted galleries that UNESCO lists at Patan. Also: Rani ki Vav kund. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/922

## Red Fort (Delhi)

## Red Fort — Diwan-i-Khas (Delhi)

- The Red Fort's Diwan-i-Khas is the marble private-audience hall on the river terrace of Shahjahanabad's palace, distinct from the public Diwan-i-Am along the same processional axis. Also: Hall of Private Audience Red Fort. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/231

## Red Fort — Lahori Gate (Delhi)

- Lahori Gate is the main western entrance of the Red Fort, opening toward the old city's Chandni Chowk axis and used for the annual Independence Day ceremony. Also: Lahori Gate. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/231

## Red Fort — Moti Masjid (Delhi)

- The Moti Masjid is a compact white-marble mosque added inside the Red Fort for imperial private worship, set apart from the public Jama Masjid of Shahjahanabad. Also: Pearl Mosque Red Fort. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/231

## Red Fort — Naubat Khana (Delhi)

- The Naubat Khana (drum house) stands on the Red Fort's processional path from Lahori Gate toward the Diwan-i-Am, where ceremonial music announced court arrivals. Also: Naqqar Khana, drum house Red Fort. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/231

## Red Fort — Rang Mahal (Delhi)

- Rang Mahal is one of the marble palace pavilions on the Red Fort's Yamuna terrace, part of the imperial zenana sequence beyond the Diwan-i-Khas. Also: Rang Mahal Red Fort. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/231

## Sanchi — Great Stupa (Sanchi)

- Sanchi Stupa 1, the Great Stupa, is the hemispherical Buddhist monument at the centre of the UNESCO sanctuary, developed from the Mauryan period with later gateways. Also: Stupa 1 Sanchi. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/524

## Sanchi — Great Stupa gateways (Sanchi)

- The Great Stupa at Sanchi is ringed by carved stone torana gateways whose reliefs narrate Buddhist stories; UNESCO identifies the sanctuary as developed from the 3rd century BCE. Also: Sanchi torana, toranas. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/524

## Sanchi — Stupa 2 (Sanchi)

- Sanchi Stupa 2, down the hill from the Great Stupa, preserves early Buddhist railing medallions and is a quieter official monument in the same UNESCO sanctuary. Also: Sanchi Stupa 2. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/524

## Taj Mahal (Agra)

- (verified) UNESCO describes the Taj Mahal as an immense white-marble mausoleum built in Agra between 1631 and 1648 by order of Mughal emperor Shah Jahan in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal. Also: Taj, Rauza-i-Munawwara. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/252
- (verified) The mausoleum stands in a vast Mughal charbagh (four-part garden) aligned on the Yamuna, a layout UNESCO cites as part of the property's outstanding architectural composition. Also: charbagh, Taj garden. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/252
- (verified) Flanking the tomb are a mosque and a matching jawab (guest house) in red sandstone, creating the bilateral symmetry that UNESCO identifies as central to the ensemble. Also: Taj mosque, jawab. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/252
- (verified) The white marble surfaces are decorated with pietra dura inlay of semi-precious stones in floral and calligraphic panels, a defining craft of the mausoleum. Also: pietra dura, inlay. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/252
- (estimated) Four minarets frame the tomb chamber; they are slightly tilted outward, a construction choice often noted so that in a collapse they would fall away from the mausoleum. Also: Taj minarets. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/252

## Taj Mahal — Great Gate (Agra)

- The red-sandstone Great Gate (Darwaza-i-Rauza) frames the first axial view of the white mausoleum across the charbagh and is itself inlaid with verses and floral decoration. Also: Darwaza-i-Rauza, Taj gateway. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/252

## Taj Mahal — Main Mausoleum (Agra)

- The central onion dome covers an octagonal tomb chamber that holds cenotaphs of Mumtaz Mahal and Shah Jahan; the actual graves lie in a lower crypt, a typical Mughal arrangement. Also: tomb chamber, cenotaphs. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/252

## Taj Mahal — Mosque and Jawab (Agra)

- The Taj ensemble's red-sandstone mosque and matching jawab (guest house) flank the white mausoleum, creating the bilateral symmetry UNESCO identifies in the garden-tomb. Also: Taj mosque hall, jawab pavilion. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/252

## Taj Mahal — Pietra dura panels (Agra)

- Inside and on the Taj mausoleum, floral pietra dura inlay of semi-precious stones covers the marble dados and cenotaph screens — the craft UNESCO associates with the tomb's decoration. Also: pietra dura Taj rooms. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/252

## Walled City of Jaipur (Jaipur)

- (verified) UNESCO records that Jaipur was founded in 1727 by Sawai Jai Singh II as a planned trade-capital with a grid of streets, public squares, and bazaars, inscribed in 2019. Also: Pink City, Sawai Jai Singh II. Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1605
