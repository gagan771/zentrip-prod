# India places and monuments

Most-visited and UNESCO-linked places across India. Each fact has a source.

## Ahmedabad

### Historic City of Ahmadabad

UNESCO lists the Historic City of Ahmadabad as a walled historic capital founded in the 15th century, with a dense fabric of wooden houses, pols, mosques, and tombs. Also known as: Ahmedabad old city, Bhadra.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1551

## Amritsar

### Golden Temple

Harmandir Sahib, the Golden Temple in Amritsar, is the principal gurdwara of Sikhism, set in the Amrit Sarovar, administered by the Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee, and open to all. Also known as: Harmandir Sahib, Sri Darbar Sahib.

Source: Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee — https://sgpc.net/

## Aurangabad

### Ajanta Caves

UNESCO lists the Ajanta Caves as Buddhist rock-cut cave monuments in Maharashtra whose paintings and sculptures are masterpieces of Buddhist religious art from roughly the 2nd century BCE to the 6th century CE. Also known as: Ajanta.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/242

### Ellora Caves

UNESCO lists Ellora as a sequence of 34 monasteries and temples cut into basalt, including the monolithic Kailasa temple, illustrating Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain faith side by side. Also known as: Kailasa Temple, Kailasanatha.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/243

### Bibi ka Maqbara monument

Bibi ka Maqbara is a late-17th-century Mughal garden tomb on the edge of Aurangabad, often visited with Ellora as a city-edge mausoleum. Also known as: Tomb of Rabia-ud-Daurani.

Source: Archaeological Survey of India — https://asi.nic.in/

### Daulatabad Fort

Daulatabad (Devagiri) is a steep hill fortress that was a medieval Deccan capital, now an ASI-protected citadel near Aurangabad. Also known as: Devagiri Fort.

Source: Archaeological Survey of India — https://asi.nic.in/

## Bekal

### Bekal Fort

Bekal Fort is a large laterite coastal fort in Kasaragod district, Kerala, a rampart looking over the Arabian Sea. Also known as: Bekal.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Bharatpur

### Keoladeo National Park

UNESCO lists Keoladeo (Bharatpur) as a man-made wetland World Heritage site and a major wintering ground for migratory waterfowl, including (historically) the Siberian crane. Also known as: Bharatpur Bird Sanctuary.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/340

### Keoladeo as Agra-Jaipur stop

Keoladeo National Park at Bharatpur is the usual nature stop on the Agra–Jaipur road, a UNESCO wetland best in the winter birding months rather than the north-Indian summer. Also known as: Bharatpur birds.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Bhubaneswar

### Lingaraja Temple

Lingaraja is the principal living temple of old Bhubaneswar, a Kalinga-style Shiva complex that still organises the historic temple town (non-Hindus may have restricted inner access). Also known as: Lingaraj.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Bikaner

### Junagarh Fort

Junagarh Fort is the main palace-fort of Bikaner, a ground-level Thar citadel of palaces and museums distinct from Jodhpur's cliff Mehrangarh. Also known as: Junagarh Bikaner.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Bodh Gaya

### Mahabodhi Temple Complex at Bodh Gaya

UNESCO lists the Mahabodhi Temple Complex as one of the four holy sites related to the life of the Buddha, marking the place of Enlightenment, with the present temple dating mainly from the 5th–6th centuries. Also known as: Bodhgaya, Bodhi Tree temple.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1056

## Chamoli

### Nanda Devi and Valley of Flowers National Parks

UNESCO lists Nanda Devi and Valley of Flowers National Parks in Uttarakhand as a high-Himalayan wilderness of outstanding alpine flora and the Nanda Devi basin. Also known as: Valley of Flowers, Nanda Devi.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/335

## Chandigarh

### Capitol Complex, Chandigarh

UNESCO includes Chandigarh's Capitol Complex among the Architectural Work of Le Corbusier, an outstanding contribution to the Modern Movement, with the Secretariat, Assembly, and High Court. Also known as: Le Corbusier Chandigarh, Open Hand.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1321

## Chittorgarh

### Chittorgarh Fort

Chittorgarh Fort is the largest in the UNESCO Hill Forts of Rajasthan series, a vast plateau fortress of the Sisodia Rajputs with towers, palaces, and water bodies. Also known as: Chittor Fort.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247

## Darjeeling

### Mountain Railways of India

UNESCO lists the Mountain Railways of India, including the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, as outstanding examples of hill railway engineering from the British colonial period that are still in use. Also known as: Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, toy train.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/944

## Delhi

### India Gate

India Gate is the All India War Memorial arch on Rajpath (Kartavya Path) in New Delhi, commemorating soldiers who died in the First World War and the Third Anglo-Afghan War. Also known as: All India War Memorial.

Source: National War Memorial / India Gate precinct, Government of India — https://www.nationalwarmemorial.gov.in/

### Lotus Temple

The Lotus Temple is the Baha'i House of Worship in New Delhi, a lotus-shaped concrete temple open to people of all religions for silent prayer and meditation. Also known as: Baha'i House of Worship Delhi.

Source: Baha'i House of Worship, New Delhi — https://www.bahaihouseofworship.in/

### Jama Masjid, Delhi

Jama Masjid in Shahjahanabad is among India's largest congregational mosques, commissioned by Shah Jahan and sited beside the Red Fort's old city. Also known as: Masjid-i-Jahan-Numa.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Sunder Nursery

Sunder Nursery is a landscaped heritage park beside Humayun's Tomb, restored as a public arboretum and tomb garden — an official quiet green, not a hidden speakeasy. Also known as: Sundar Nursery.

Source: Archaeological Survey of India — https://asi.nic.in/

### Agrasen ki Baoli monument

Agrasen ki Baoli is a historic stepwell in central Delhi protected as a monument, a short official stop rather than a hidden nightclub. Also known as: Agrasen Baoli.

Source: Archaeological Survey of India — https://asi.nic.in/

## Goa

### Fort Aguada

Fort Aguada is a 17th-century Portuguese coastal fort and watering point on Goa's Bardez headland, with a lighthouse precinct overlooking the Arabian Sea. Also known as: Aguada fortress.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Guwahati

### Kamakhya Temple

Kamakhya on Nilachal Hill is Guwahati's principal Shakti pilgrimage temple, a living shrine with festival crowds (especially Ambubachi) and hill-road access from the Brahmaputra city. Also known as: Kamakhya.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Hampi

### Group of Monuments at Hampi

UNESCO lists Hampi as the ruined capital of the Vijayanagara Empire, with temple, palace, and fort remains along the Tungabhadra, including the Virupaksha temple still in worship. Also known as: Vijayanagara, Virupaksha Temple.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/241

## Hyderabad

### Charminar

Charminar is the 1591 ceremonial monument and mosque at the centre of Hyderabad's old city, built by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, with four grand arches facing the cardinal bazaars. Also known as: Four Minarets.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Golconda Fort

Golconda Fort is the ruined Qutb Shahi citadel west of Hyderabad, known for its acoustics along the gates and as the predecessor capital before the Charminar city. Also known as: Golkonda.

Source: Archaeological Survey of India — https://asi.nic.in/

### Qutb Shahi Tombs

The Qutb Shahi tomb garden near Golconda is a necropolis of the dynasty that founded Hyderabad, a quieter official garden-tomb field than Charminar's bazaar. Also known as: Ibrahim Bagh tombs.

Source: Archaeological Survey of India — https://asi.nic.in/

## Jaisalmer

### Jaisalmer Fort

Jaisalmer Fort is one of the six Hill Forts of Rajasthan on the UNESCO list: a living sandstone fort-city in the Thar Desert with palaces, Jain temples, and merchants' havelis inside the walls. Also known as: Sonar Quila, Golden Fort.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247

## Jodhpur

### Mehrangarh Fort

Mehrangarh is the principal fort of Jodhpur, a massive cliff palace-fort above the blue old town, with museums in the palatial apartments and a wide Thar panorama from the ramparts. Also known as: Mehrangarh.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Kanyakumari

### Vivekananda Rock Memorial

Vivekananda Rock Memorial is a ticketed memorial on a rock off Kanyakumari, reached by ferry when sea conditions allow — separate from the mainland confluence viewpoint. Also known as: Vivekananda Rock.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Kaziranga

### Kaziranga National Park

UNESCO lists Kaziranga in Assam as a natural World Heritage site on the Brahmaputra floodplain, the world's major habitat of the greater one-horned rhinoceros. Also known as: one-horned rhinoceros.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/337

## Kevadia

### Statue of Unity

The Statue of Unity on the Narmada in Gujarat is a colossal statue of Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel developed as a national memorial and visitor destination by the Government of Gujarat. Also known as: Sardar Patel statue.

Source: Statue of Unity, Government of Gujarat — https://www.statueofunity.in/

## Khajuraho

### Khajuraho Group of Monuments

UNESCO lists the Khajuraho Group of Monuments as a surviving cluster of Chandela-period Hindu and Jain temples in Madhya Pradesh, known for Nagara architecture and sculptural programmes. Also known as: Khajuraho temples.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/240

## Kolkata

### Victoria Memorial

The Victoria Memorial in Kolkata is a white-marble museum-memorial completed in the early 20th century in memory of Queen Victoria, now a national museum of the city and the Raj period. Also known as: Victoria Memorial Hall.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Konark

### Sun Temple, Konark

UNESCO lists the Sun Temple at Konark as a 13th-century Kalinga temple conceived as a colossal chariot of the sun god Surya, with 24 carved wheels and pulling horses. Also known as: Konarak, Black Pagoda.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/246

## Kumbhalgarh

### Kumbhalgarh Fort

Kumbhalgarh is a UNESCO Hill Fort of Rajasthan whose perimeter walls run for tens of kilometres across the Aravalli hills, enclosing palaces and temples. Also known as: Kumbhalgarh wall.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247

## Lucknow

### Bara Imambara

Bara Imambara is Lucknow's monumental Nawabi congregational hall with the bhool bhulaiya labyrinth, a ticketed historic complex rather than a cafe rooftop. Also known as: Asafi Imambara, Bhulbhulaiya.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Madurai

### Meenakshi Amman Temple

Meenakshi Amman Temple in Madurai is a living Dravidian temple complex dedicated to Meenakshi and Sundareswarar, famed for its towering painted gopurams on the Vaigai river city. Also known as: Meenakshi Temple, Madurai Meenakshi.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Mahabalipuram

### Group of Monuments at Mahabalipuram

UNESCO lists Mahabalipuram as a group of 7th–8th century Pallava rock-cut rathas, cave temples, sculpted reliefs, and the Shore Temple on the Tamil Nadu coast. Also known as: Mamallapuram, Shore Temple.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/249

## Mumbai

### Gateway of India

The Gateway of India on Mumbai Harbour is a 20th-century basalt arch built to commemorate the 1911 visit of King George V and Queen Mary, and later became the ceremonial harbour landmark of Bombay. Also known as: Apollo Bunder.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Elephanta Caves

UNESCO lists the Elephanta Caves as a rock-cut temple complex on an island in Mumbai Harbour, famous for the colossal Trimurti sculpture of Shiva. Also known as: Gharapuri.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/244

### Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus

UNESCO describes Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus (formerly Victoria Terminus) as an outstanding example of Victorian Gothic Revival architecture in India, still a working railway terminus. Also known as: CST, Victoria Terminus, VT.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/945

### Kanheri Caves monument

Kanheri is a large Buddhist cave complex in Sanjay Gandhi National Park, Mumbai, an ASI site inside the city forest distinct from the harbour island of Elephanta. Also known as: Kanheri Caves SGNP.

Source: Archaeological Survey of India — https://asi.nic.in/

## Mysuru

### Mysore Palace

Mysore Palace (Amba Vilas) is the official seat of the Wadiyars and a major Indo-Saracenic palace complex whose illuminated Dasara season is one of Karnataka's principal visitor events. Also known as: Amba Vilas, Mysuru Palace.

Source: Mysore Palace Board — https://mysorepalace.gov.in/

## Old Goa

### Churches and Convents of Goa

UNESCO lists the Churches and Convents of Goa as the former capital of the Portuguese Indies, including the Basilica of Bom Jesus which houses the relics of St Francis Xavier. Also known as: Basilica of Bom Jesus, Se Cathedral.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/234

## Omkareshwar

### Omkareshwar Temple

Omkareshwar is a Jyotirlinga temple town on a Narmada island (Mandhata), a living pilgrimage rather than a palace ruin. Also known as: Omkareshwar Mandhata.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Orchha

### Orchha Fort-Palace

Orchha's fort-palace and Betwa cenotaphs are the Bundela capital's principal monuments, a compact riverside ensemble staged from Jhansi. Also known as: Raja Mahal Orchha.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Patan

### Rani-ki-Vav

UNESCO lists Rani-ki-Vav at Patan, Gujarat, as an 11th-century inverted-temple stepwell built in the Maru-Gurjara style, with sculptural levels dedicated primarily to Vishnu. Also known as: Queen's Stepwell.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/922

## Pattadakal

### Group of Monuments at Pattadakal

UNESCO lists Pattadakal in Karnataka as a 7th–8th century Chalukya sacred ensemble on the Malaprabha, blending northern and southern Indian temple forms. Also known as: Pattadakal.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/239

## Port Blair

### Cellular Jail

Cellular Jail in Port Blair is the colonial prison memorial of the Andamans, now a national memorial museum with a harbour-side setting. Also known as: Kalapani.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Pune

### Shaniwar Wada

Shaniwar Wada is the ruined palace-fort of the Peshwas in Pune's old city, an ASI-protected precinct rather than a complete standing palace like Mysuru. Also known as: Shaniwarwada.

Source: Archaeological Survey of India — https://asi.nic.in/

## Puri

### Jagannath Temple Puri

Jagannath Temple in Puri is one of Hinduism's Char Dham shrines, a living temple-city on the Odisha coast; inner-sanctum access follows temple rules and is not a museum walk. Also known as: Sri Jagannath, Puri temple.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Raisen

### Bhimbetka Rock Shelters

Bhimbetka is a cluster of Vindhyan rock shelters with prehistoric paintings, an ASI-protected landscape near Bhopal and a UNESCO World Heritage property. Also known as: Bhimbetka.

Source: Archaeological Survey of India — https://asi.nic.in/

## Rameswaram

### Ramanathaswamy Temple

Ramanathaswamy Temple on Rameswaram island is a major Shaiva pilgrimage shrine with long corridors, reached across the Pamban bridge from the Tamil mainland. Also known as: Rameswaram temple.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Ranakpur

### Ranakpur Jain Temple

The marble Jain temple at Ranakpur in the Aravallis is a major pilgrimage and visitor temple on the Udaipur–Kumbhalgarh road, with extensive carved pillars. Also known as: Chaunukha Temple.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Sanchi

### Buddhist Monuments at Sanchi

UNESCO lists Sanchi as the oldest Buddhist sanctuary in existence, developed from the 3rd century BCE, whose Great Stupa and gateways are among India's finest early Buddhist art. Also known as: Sanchi Stupa, Great Stupa.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/524

## Thanjavur

### Great Living Chola Temples

UNESCO's Great Living Chola Temples include the Brihadisvara Temple at Thanjavur, a living 11th-century Chola imperial temple of granite with a towering vimana. Also known as: Brihadisvara, Brihadeeswara, Rajarajesvaram.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage List — https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/250

## Udaipur

### Sajjangarh

Sajjangarh (Monsoon Palace) is a hilltop 19th-century palace above Udaipur used as a dusk viewpoint over the lakes; it is a ticketed site on a winding hill road, not a cafe. Also known as: Monsoon Palace.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Varanasi

### Kashi Vishwanath Temple

Shri Kashi Vishwanath is a principal Jyotirlinga temple of Shiva on the Ganga in Varanasi, one of Hinduism's holiest tirthas, with the present shrine rebuilt in the 18th century and a major visitor corridor beside the river. Also known as: Golden Temple Varanasi, Jyotirlinga Kashi.

Source: Shri Kashi Vishwanath Temple — https://shrikashivishwanath.org/

### Sarnath

Sarnath, north of Varanasi, is the site of the Buddha's first sermon; the Archaeological Survey of India protects the Dhamek Stupa, monasteries, and the museum that holds the Lion Capital of Ashoka. Also known as: Deer Park, Dhamek Stupa.

Source: Archaeological Survey of India — https://asi.nic.in/
