# Travel operations: cities, seasons, routes, food districts

Planning guidance. Distances and seasons are typical, not live ETAs or weather.

## City Guide

### Delhi visitor primer — Delhi

(estimated) Delhi is the National Capital Territory and a hub for the Golden Triangle. Core visitor clusters are Shahjahanabad (Red Fort, Jama Masjid, Chandni Chowk), the Lutyens central vista (India Gate), South Delhi's Qutb and Humayun's Tomb, and the airport/Aerocity belt.

Also: New Delhi, NCR.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Agra visitor primer — Agra

(estimated) Agra's visitor core is the Yamuna monument belt: Taj Mahal, Agra Fort, Mehtab Bagh on the opposite bank, and Fatehpur Sikri about 40 km west. Peak monument hours are crowded; ASI sites have timed entry rules that change — check the official ticket window.

Also: Agra city.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Jaipur visitor primer — Jaipur

(estimated) Jaipur's walled city (bazaars, City Palace, Hawa Mahal, Jantar Mantar) sits below the Amber–Nahargarh–Jaigarh hill-fort chain. The planned 1727 grid makes the old city walkable; Amber is a separate hill trip northeast of town.

Also: Pink City primer.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Varanasi visitor primer — Varanasi

(estimated) Varanasi is a Ganga pilgrimage city. The riverfront ghats, Kashi Vishwanath corridor, and Sarnath (about 10 km north) are the three visitor poles. Ghat stairs and monsoon water levels change access; boats are unregulated compared with ASI monuments.

Also: Benares, Kashi.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Mumbai visitor primer — Mumbai

(estimated) Mumbai visitor poles include the Colaba–Apollo Bunder waterfront (Gateway of India, ferry to Elephanta), the Fort/CST UNESCO precinct, Marine Drive, and the island caves. Monsoon (June–September) brings heavy rain and ferry cancellations.

Also: Bombay.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Goa visitor primer — Goa

(estimated) Goa combines beach talukas with the UNESCO Churches and Convents of Old Goa inland. Peak dry season is roughly November–February; the southwest monsoon (June–September) closes much of the beach-season economy.

Also: Old Goa.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Udaipur visitor primer — Udaipur

(estimated) Udaipur is organised around Lake Pichola and the City Palace waterfront, with hill views from Sajjangarh (Monsoon Palace) and the nearby Kumbhalgarh–Ranakpur circuit in the Aravallis.

Also: City of Lakes.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Jodhpur visitor primer — Jodhpur

(estimated) Jodhpur's core is Mehrangarh Fort above the blue-washed old town, with the surrounding Thar approach toward Jaisalmer and the Umaid Bhawan museum palace on the city edge.

Also: Blue City.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Amritsar visitor primer — Amritsar

(estimated) Amritsar's visitor core is the Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple) complex and the nearby Wagah border ceremony. The langar is open to all; dress codes apply inside the gurdwara.

Also: Amritsar city.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Leh visitor primer — Leh

(estimated) Leh is a high-altitude desert town (about 3,500 m). The practical visitor season is roughly June–September when roads from Manali and Srinagar are usually open; AMS risk requires a rest day on arrival.

Also: Ladakh.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Bengaluru visitor primer — Bengaluru

(estimated) Bengaluru is a year-round aviation hub for south India (Hampi, Mysuru, coastal Karnataka) with a milder climate than the Deccan summer; it is a transit city more than a monument city.

Also: Bangalore.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Kochi visitor primer — Kochi

(estimated) Kochi (Fort Kochi, Mattancherry, Ernakulam) is the usual air/rail gateway to central Kerala backwaters and Munnar. The southwest monsoon is June–September.

Also: Cochin, Ernakulam.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Hyderabad visitor primer — Hyderabad

(estimated) Hyderabad's old city is organised around Charminar and the Laad Bazaar, with Golconda Fort and the Qutb Shahi tombs on the western edge and the newer Hi-Tech/airport south.

Also: Golconda city.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Kolkata visitor primer — Kolkata

(estimated) Kolkata visitor poles include the Maidan–Victoria Memorial, Howrah riverfront, north Kolkata colonial/temple streets, and the Sundarbans further south-east as a separate overnight trip.

Also: Calcutta.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Chennai visitor primer — Chennai

(estimated) Chennai is the rail/air gateway to the Tamil temple coast: Mahabalipuram is about an hour south, with Madurai, Thanjavur, and Rameswaram as further Tamil Nadu legs. Hot season is typically April–June.

Also: Madras.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Kanyakumari visitor primer — Kanyakumari

(estimated) Kanyakumari is the Tamil Nadu tip town for sunrise/sunset over the confluence and the Vivekananda Rock ferry. It is a short-stay pilgrimage/viewpoint stop, not a beach-resort belt like Goa.

Also: Cape Comorin.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Madurai visitor primer — Madurai

(estimated) Madurai's core is the Meenakshi–Sundareswarar temple and the surrounding bazaar streets on the Vaigai. It is a living temple city: early inner-sanctum rules, dress codes, and festival crowds dominate the calendar.

Also: Madurai city.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Mysuru visitor primer — Mysuru

(estimated) Mysuru is organised around the palace, Chamundi Hill, and the Dasara season. It is a gentler Deccan city than Bengaluru traffic, used as a palace-and-market overnight from the Bengaluru hub.

Also: Mysore city.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Ahmedabad visitor primer — Ahmedabad

(estimated) Ahmedabad's visitor core is the UNESCO historic city (pols, Bhadra, riverfront) plus Sabarmati-related sites. Heat is the planning constraint; Rani-ki-Vav at Patan and the Statue of Unity are separate day trips.

Also: Amdavad.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Bhubaneswar visitor primer — Bhubaneswar

(estimated) Bhubaneswar is the air/rail hub for Odisha's coastal triangle: old-town Lingaraja temples, plus Puri and Konark as half-day hops. Summer is hot; cyclone watches matter in the post-monsoon Bay season.

Also: Temple City Odisha.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Aurangabad visitor primer — Aurangabad

(estimated) Aurangabad (Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar) is the practical base for Ellora (close) and Ajanta (a longer day). Daulatabad and Bibi ka Maqbara are city-edge add-ons, not substitutes for the cave UNESCO sites.

Also: Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Pune visitor primer — Pune

(estimated) Pune is a Deccan education-and-cantonment city used as a Mumbai expressway hop. Visitor poles include the old city, Shaniwar Wada precinct, and hill forts further out — not a harbour monument city.

Also: Poona.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Shimla visitor primer — Shimla

(estimated) Shimla is a Himalayan ridge town: Mall Road, the Ridge, and the Kalka–Shimla mountain railway. It is a hill-station climate escape from the north Indian plains, with monsoon landslide risk on the highway.

Also: Simla.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Darjeeling visitor primer — Darjeeling

(estimated) Darjeeling is a ridge town for Kanchenjunga views, tea estates, and the Himalayan railway. Clear-view mornings are weather-dependent; the toy train is a booked heritage ride, not a city metro.

Also: Dorje Ling.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Rishikesh visitor primer — Rishikesh

(estimated) Rishikesh sits where the Ganga leaves the Himalaya: ghats, ashrams, and the Lakshman Jhula/Ram Jhula crossings. It is a pilgrimage and adventure-sports town; monsoon river levels and hill-road closures are the constraints.

Also: Yoga capital.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Puducherry visitor primer — Puducherry

(estimated) Puducherry's visitor core is the French Quarter grid, the seaside promenade, and nearby Auroville as a separate township. It is a coastal add-on from Chennai (~150 km), not a temple-circuit substitute for Mahabalipuram.

Also: Pondicherry, Pondy.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Srinagar visitor primer — Srinagar

(estimated) Srinagar is organised around Dal and Nagin lakes, the Mughal gardens, and the old city. It is the western air/road gateway toward Sonamarg and the Srinagar–Leh highway when that road is open. Check current official travel advisories before planning.

Also: Srinagar city, Dal Lake.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Manali visitor primer — Manali

(estimated) Manali is a Beas-valley hill town used as the jumping-off point for Rohtang/Atal Tunnel toward Keylong and the Leh highway in season. It is not a substitute for Leh itself; monsoon landslides and winter closures are the planning constraints.

Also: Manali Kullu.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Dharamshala visitor primer — Dharamshala

(estimated) Dharamshala / McLeod Ganj is the Tibetan-exile hill seat above Kangra, with the Tsuglagkhang complex and ridge walks toward Triund. It is a Himachal monsoon-and-winter hill town, not a desert fort circuit.

Also: McLeod Ganj, McLeodganj.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Gangtok visitor primer — Gangtok

(estimated) Gangtok is Sikkim's ridge capital and the permit/tourism hub for Tsomgo (Changu) Lake and further north-Sikkim circuits. Inner-line / protected-area permits apply to many side valleys; confirm Sikkim Tourism rules before travel.

Also: Sikkim capital.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Shillong visitor primer — Shillong

(estimated) Shillong is Meghalaya's plateau capital, used as the base for Sohra (Cherrapunji) and the wettest-hills circuit. The southwest monsoon is intense; cloud often hides Shillong Peak views.

Also: Meghalaya capital.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Bikaner visitor primer — Bikaner

(estimated) Bikaner sits on the Thar north of Jodhpur, organised around Junagarh Fort and the old walled city. It is a desert overnight on Jaipur–Jaisalmer or Jodhpur circuits; summer midday heat matches Jaisalmer.

Also: Junagarh city.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Pushkar visitor primer — Pushkar

(estimated) Pushkar is a small pilgrimage town around a holy lake, a short hop from Ajmer. The camel fair (typically October/November, dates shift with the lunar calendar) is the peak crowd; the lake ghats remain a religious space year-round.

Also: Pushkar lake.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Guwahati visitor primer — Guwahati

(estimated) Guwahati on the Brahmaputra is the air/rail gateway to Kaziranga, Shillong, and much of the Northeast. Kamakhya Temple is the city's principal pilgrimage site; river flooding and monsoon humidity define summer–autumn.

Also: Gauhati.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Puri visitor primer — Puri

(estimated) Puri is Odisha's pilgrimage-and-beach town: Jagannath Temple, Grand Road, and the Bay of Bengal strand. Konark is a short coastal hop; cyclones and temple festival calendars dominate planning more than winter fog.

Also: Jagannath Puri.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Sundarbans visitor primer — Sundarbans

(estimated) The Sundarbans are a mangrove tiger reserve on the India–Bangladesh delta, reached as an overnight from Kolkata via Godkhali/jetty circuits. Permits and boat rules are forest-department controlled; this is not a city monument walk.

Also: Sundarban.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Shantiniketan visitor primer — Bolpur

(estimated) Santiniketan (Bolpur) is Visva-Bharati's university town in Birbhum, a cultural overnight from Kolkata associated with Tagore. It is a campus-and-ashram landscape, not a beach or fort circuit.

Also: Santiniketan.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Gokarna visitor primer — Gokarna

(estimated) Gokarna is a Karnataka temple-and-cove town, quieter than Goa, used for Om Beach and the Mahabaleshwar temple. The southwest monsoon shuts much of the beach-hut season; it is a Konkan hop from Goa or Hubballi, not a substitute for Hampi.

Also: Gokarna beaches.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Panaji visitor primer — Panaji

(estimated) Panaji (Panjim) is Goa's capital on the Mandovi, the usual base for Old Goa's UNESCO churches (about 10 km) and a calmer alternative to north-Goa beach strips. Monsoon still applies.

Also: Panjim.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Thanjavur visitor primer — Thanjavur

(estimated) Thanjavur is the Chola imperial temple town around Brihadisvara, with the palace museum and bronze-craft streets. Combine with Gangaikonda Cholapuram and Darasuram as short delta hops, not a single ticket.

Also: Tanjore.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Rameswaram visitor primer — Rameswaram

(estimated) Rameswaram is a pilgrimage island-town on Pamban, linked by the sea bridge from the Tamil mainland. Ramanathaswamy Temple and the coastal ghats are the core; cyclone and monsoon sea states affect the causeway mood.

Also: Pamban.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Hampi visitor primer — Hampi

(estimated) Hampi is a spread-out Vijayanagara ruin field on the Tungabhadra; Hosapete is the rail/stay gateway. Bicycle or auto between temple clusters; there is little shade. October–February is the kinder walking season.

Also: Hosapete base.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Mahabalipuram visitor primer — Mahabalipuram

(estimated) Mahabalipuram is a compact Pallava coastal town: Shore Temple, rathas, and the hill reliefs, about an hour from Chennai on the ECR. It is a half-day or overnight, not a separate flight from the Tamil interior.

Also: Mahabs town.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Lucknow visitor primer — Lucknow

(estimated) Lucknow's visitor poles are the Nawabi imambaras and gardens (Bara Imambara, Chhota Imambara, Rumi Darwaza) plus the Gomti riverfront. Heat matches the north Indian plains; Chowk/Aminabad is the food walking district.

Also: Awadh.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Bhopal visitor primer — Bhopal

(estimated) Bhopal is the practical air/rail gateway to Sanchi (about 50 km) and a lakeside capital in its own right. It is not a substitute for Khajuraho, which is a separate plateau hop.

Also: Sanchi gateway city.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Alibag visitor primer — Alibag

(estimated) Alibag is the mainland Konkan town opposite Mumbai Harbour, reached by ferry from the Gateway in fair weather or by the longer road via Pen. Kolaba Fort sits in the tidal creek; monsoon cancels many ferries.

Also: Alibaug.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Udupi visitor primer — Udupi

(estimated) Udupi is a coastal Karnataka temple town (Krishna Matha) and a Konkan Railway stop between Mangaluru and Goa. It is a pilgrimage-and-meal-stop, not a substitute for Hampi's ruin field.

Also: Udupi temple town.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Mangaluru visitor primer — Mangaluru

(estimated) Mangaluru is the southern Karnataka port-and-temple gateway (Kadri, St Aloysius chapel precinct, harbour). It is the usual air/rail hinge between Kerala and the Konkan, with a heavy southwest monsoon.

Also: Mangalore.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Diu visitor primer — Diu

(estimated) Diu is a small island union-territory town with a Portuguese-era fort and churches, drier than Goa and reached via Veraval/Una in Gujarat. Alcohol rules differ from Gujarat state; it is a short coastal add-on, not a Golden Triangle hop.

Also: Diu island.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Murud-Janjira note — Murud

(estimated) Murud-Janjira is a sea fort off the Raigad coast, reached by boat from Rajapuri when the sea allows. It is a Deccan-sultanate island citadel, not a Mumbai neighbourhood cafe strip.

Also: Janjira Fort.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Tiruchirappalli visitor primer — Tiruchirappalli

(estimated) Tiruchirappalli (Trichy) is the Kaveri-delta air/rail hinge: Srirangam's Ranganathaswamy temple-island, Rockfort, and a short hop toward Thanjavur's Brihadisvara. It is a living temple city, not a beach stop.

Also: Trichy, Srirangam.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Kanchipuram visitor primer — Kanchipuram

(estimated) Kanchipuram is a Tamil temple-and-silk town west of Chennai, with large living Shaiva and Vaishnava complexes. It is a half-day or overnight from Chennai, distinct from Mahabalipuram's Pallava coast.

Also: Kanchi.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Madikeri visitor primer — Madikeri

(estimated) Madikeri is the Kodagu (Coorg) hill-district headquarters: coffee country and misty ghats, reached from Bengaluru or Mysuru. Monsoon is heavy; it is not a Hampi boulder field.

Also: Coorg, Kodagu.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Munnar visitor primer — Munnar

(estimated) Munnar is a Western Ghats tea-town in Idukki, the usual hill overnight from Kochi. Roads are winding; the southwest monsoon makes ghats slippery. Eravikulam (Nilgiri tahr) is a separate national-park ticket when open.

Also: Kannan Devan hills.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Thekkady visitor primer — Thekkady

(estimated) Thekkady/Kumily is the visitor edge of Periyar Tiger Reserve: boat on the reservoir and spice-village streets. Forest-department rules apply; monsoon affects boat operations.

Also: Periyar, Kumily.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Thiruvananthapuram visitor primer — Thiruvananthapuram

(estimated) Thiruvananthapuram is Kerala's capital and air gateway, with Padmanabhaswamy Temple in the old city and Kovalam as the nearby beach strip. Temple dress and entry rules are strict; Kovalam is a separate coastal pocket.

Also: Trivandrum, Kovalam.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Varkala visitor primer — Varkala

(estimated) Varkala is a cliff-and-beach pilgrimage town north of Thiruvananthapuram (Janardhana Temple and the cliff promenade). Monsoon swell makes the cliff edge dangerous; it is not Fort Kochi's harbour heritage.

Also: Varkala cliff.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Halebidu Belur primer — Halebidu

(estimated) Belur and Halebidu are paired Hoysala temple towns in Hassan district, a day from Mysuru or Bengaluru. They are compact sculptural temples, quieter than Hampi's spread-out capital, with ASI/state tickets.

Also: Belur Halebid, Hoysala temples.

Source: Archaeological Survey of India — https://asi.nic.in/

### Badami visitor primer — Badami

(estimated) Badami's cave temples and the nearby Aihole–Pattadakal field are the Early Chalukya circuit on the Malaprabha, often staged from Hubballi or as a long add-on from Hampi. Heat on the sandstone is severe in April–May.

Also: Aihole, Malaprabha.

Source: Archaeological Survey of India — https://asi.nic.in/

### Indore visitor primer — Indore

(estimated) Indore is a Malwa commercial city and the usual staging point for Ujjain's temples and Mandu's plateau palaces. It is not itself a UNESCO monument town; summer heat on the plateau is severe.

Also: Indore city.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Gwalior visitor primer — Gwalior

(estimated) Gwalior is organised around its massive hill fort, palaces, and the Sas-Bahu temples, a separate Madhya Pradesh stop from Khajuraho. It sits on the Delhi–Mumbai rail spine.

Also: Gwalior Fort city.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Prayagraj visitor primer — Prayagraj

(estimated) Prayagraj (Allahabad) is the Ganga–Yamuna sangam city, with the fort and river confluence as the visitor core. Kumbh-calendar crowds dwarf ordinary days; it is a plains hop from Varanasi or Lucknow.

Also: Allahabad, Sangam.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Nashik visitor primer — Nashik

(estimated) Nashik is a Godavari pilgrimage and vineyard-plateau city, with Trimbakeshwar as a nearby Jyotirlinga. It is a few hours from Mumbai/Pune, not a Konkan beach town.

Also: Nasik, Trimbakeshwar.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Vadodara visitor primer — Vadodara

(estimated) Vadodara (Baroda) is a Gujarat plains city with Laxmi Vilas Palace and a useful rail hinge between Ahmedabad and Mumbai. The Statue of Unity is a separate Narmada hop via Kevadia, not a city-centre walk.

Also: Baroda.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Visakhapatnam visitor primer — Visakhapatnam

(estimated) Visakhapatnam is the north Andhra port-and-beach city: RK Beach, Kailasagiri ridge, and the Eastern Ghats hinterland toward Araku. Cyclone watches on the Bay matter in the post-monsoon months.

Also: Vizag, Waltair.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Nagpur visitor primer — Nagpur

(estimated) Nagpur is the central-Indian rail/air crossroads for Tadoba and Pench tiger reserves and a hinge between Mumbai, Kolkata, and Hyderabad. It is a transit city more than a monument city.

Also: Orange city.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Dehradun visitor primer — Dehradun

(estimated) Dehradun is the Doon-valley gateway to Mussoorie and the Yamuna/Ganga Himalayan approaches. It is a plains-edge city; monsoon landslides affect the Mussoorie road.

Also: Doon valley.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Jammu visitor primer — Jammu

(estimated) Jammu is the winter-capital railhead and the usual base for the Vaishno Devi pilgrimage (Katra). Srinagar is a separate Kashmir-valley flight or highway; check current advisories.

Also: Jammu city, Vaishno Devi gateway.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Port Blair visitor primer — Port Blair

(estimated) Port Blair is the Andaman air/sea gateway: Cellular Jail, the harbour, and onward ferries to Havelock (Swaraj Dweep) and Neil. Permits and cyclone/monsoon ferry cancellations are the planning constraints. It is not a Kerala backwater.

Also: Sri Vijaya Puram, Andaman capital.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Mussoorie visitor primer — Mussoorie

(estimated) Mussoorie is a ridge hill-station above Dehradun (Mall Road, Gun Hill, Camel's Back). It is a short Himalayan escape from the plains, with monsoon landslide risk on the approach.

Also: Queen of Hills.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Ujjain visitor primer — Ujjain

(estimated) Ujjain is a Shipra pilgrimage city around Mahakaleshwar, a Jyotirlinga temple town staged from Indore. Simhastha/Kumbh calendars create extreme crowds; ordinary days are a temple-city visit.

Also: Mahakaleshwar.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Mandu visitor primer — Mandu

(estimated) Mandu (Mandav) is a monsoon-plateau ruin field of Afghan palaces and tanks above the Narmada scarp, a day from Indore. It is an ASI/state historic landscape, quieter than Agra, with little shade in summer.

Also: Mandav.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Kozhikode visitor primer — Kozhikode

(estimated) Kozhikode (Calicut) is a Malabar port city with beach road and spice-trade old quarters, a rail/air hinge between Kochi and Mangaluru. The southwest monsoon is intense on this coast.

Also: Calicut.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Orchha visitor primer — Orchha

(estimated) Orchha is a compact Bundela palace-and-cenotaph town on the Betwa, usually staged from Jhansi. It is quieter than Khajuraho and a half-day or overnight of riverside chhatris and the Raja Mahal, not a cave circuit.

Also: Orchha palaces.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Jhansi visitor primer — Jhansi

(estimated) Jhansi is the Bundelkhand rail hinge for Orchha and Khajuraho. The fort is a historic citadel; most visitors use the junction rather than lingering as a monument city.

Also: Jhansi fort city.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Jabalpur visitor primer — Jabalpur

(estimated) Jabalpur is the Narmada-city gateway to Bhedaghat's Marble Rocks boat gorge. Kanha and Bandhavgarh are separate overnight forest hops, not city parks.

Also: Marble Rocks, Bhedaghat.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Pachmarhi visitor primer — Pachmarhi

(estimated) Pachmarhi is Madhya Pradesh's Satpura hill-station (waterfalls, caves, Dhupgarh views), cooler than the plateau cities. Monsoon makes ghat roads slippery.

Also: Satpura hills.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

### Ayodhya visitor primer — Ayodhya

(estimated) Ayodhya is a Sarayu pilgrimage city. Visitor movement concentrates on the ram-janmabhoomi corridor and river ghats; festival days are extremely crowded. It is a plains hop from Lucknow, not a Himalayan shrine.

Also: Saketa.

Source: Ministry of Tourism, Government of India — https://tourism.gov.in/

## Season

### North India plains season — India

(estimated) For the north Indian plains (Delhi, Agra, Jaipur, Varanasi), the most comfortable visitor window is typically October–March. April–June is extreme dry heat; July–September is monsoon humidity with occasional monument-garden closures after rain.

Also: when to visit Delhi, when to visit Agra, when to visit Jaipur, winter in north India.

Source: India Meteorological Department — https://mausam.imd.gov.in/

### Rajasthan desert season — India

(estimated) Western Rajasthan (Jodhpur, Jaisalmer) is best walked October–March. April–June daytime heat is dangerous for fort climbs; nights in December–January can drop near freezing in the desert.

Also: when to visit Jaisalmer, when to visit Jodhpur.

Source: India Meteorological Department — https://mausam.imd.gov.in/

### Goa and Konkan season — Goa

(estimated) Goa's dry visitor season is typically November–February. The southwest monsoon (June–September) brings heavy rain, rough seas, and ferry/beach-shack shutdowns — still green and quieter, but not classic beach weather.

Also: when to visit Goa, Goa monsoon.

Source: India Meteorological Department — https://mausam.imd.gov.in/

### Kerala season — Kochi

(estimated) Kerala is humid year-round. A common visitor window is October–March. The southwest monsoon (June–September) is intense on the coast and in the ghats; backwater life continues but outdoor treks get slippery.

Also: when to visit Kerala, Kerala monsoon.

Source: India Meteorological Department — https://mausam.imd.gov.in/

### Ladakh season — Leh

(estimated) Ladakh's main visitor season is roughly June–September when the Manali and Srinagar roads are usually open. Winter is severe and many hotels close; altitude is a health constraint in every season.

Also: when to visit Ladakh, Leh roads.

Source: India Meteorological Department — https://mausam.imd.gov.in/

### Himalayan trek season — India

(estimated) Uttarakhand hill/trek corridors (including Valley of Flowers) are typically used May–June and September–October, subject to official trail openings. Monsoon (July–August) brings landslide risk on hill roads.

Also: when to visit Uttarakhand, Valley of Flowers season.

Source: India Meteorological Department — https://mausam.imd.gov.in/

### Kaziranga season — Kaziranga

(estimated) Kaziranga's usual safari season is roughly November–April when floodwaters recede. The monsoon floods large parts of the park; many lodges close. Confirm Assam Forest Department dates each year.

Also: when to visit Kaziranga.

Source: India Meteorological Department — https://mausam.imd.gov.in/

### Tamil Nadu temple-coast season — Chennai

(estimated) The Tamil Nadu coast and temple towns are visit-able year-round but are hottest in April–June. The northeast monsoon can bring heavy rain to Chennai/Mahabalipuram around October–December.

### Hampi heat warning — Hampi

(estimated) Hampi is an exposed boulder landscape. October–February is the kinder walking season; April–May rock surfaces become extremely hot. Carry water; there is little shade between temple clusters.
